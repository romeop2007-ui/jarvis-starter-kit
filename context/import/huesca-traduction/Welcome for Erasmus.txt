Welcome, Erasmus student, to the ‘Fundamentals of Marketing’ module on the Bachelor’s
Degree in Business Administration and Management at the University of Zaragoza, Huesca
Campus.
My name is María José Barlés Arizón, and I am the lecturer for this module
(mjbarles@unizar.es). My English isn’t very good, but I’ll do my best to make things as easy
as possible for you on this module. I will be using the DeepL translator for written
materials; I think it works quite well.
Although the course syllabus in English is available on our website (fegp.unizar.es), I’d like
to give you a brief overview of the course:
The aim of ‘Fundamentals of Marketing’ is to introduce students to the basic concepts,
tools and decisions involved in marketing and help them become familiar with them.
To this end, the course programme will be as follows:
SYLLABUS
TOPIC 1. – INTRODUCTION TO MARKETING
TOPIC 2. – PRODUCT DECISIONS
TOPIC 3. – PRICING DECISIONS
TOPIC 4. – DISTRIBUTION DECISIONS
TOPIC 5. – COMMUNICATION DECISIONS
For each topic, I will upload an outline to Moodle, which you are advised to supplement
with the recommended reading list (see the teaching guide).
The course is assessed as follows:
.
- PART 1 (7 marks; to be eligible for Part 2, you must achieve a minimum of 2.75 marks),
which consists of a final written examination to be held on 21 December 2016 as part of
the REGULAR assessment period. If you fail this or do not sit the exam on that date, you
may sit it during the SUPPLEMENTARY assessment period on 20 January 2017. The written
exam will be in English and may also be answered in English.
.
- PART 2 (3 marks). There are two options, which are not mutually exclusive:
.
- to answer supplementary questions on the same dates as the final written exam
.
- to complete and hand in the practical assignments, which will take place every
Wednesday from 8.30 am to 10.30 am for Group B (surnames starting with H or later) and
from 10.30 am to 12.30 pm for Group A (surnames from A to G). There will be individual
practical sessions (1 mark) and group practical sessions (2 marks). This option is
recommended if you are comfortable using Spanish. Practical work carried out outside
the classroom will not be accepted.
