3. Evolution in the orientation of companies towards
Marketing:
Production Orientation
• Stable and controllable environment: Demand GREATER than supply
• Assumption: People choose the available products (what is there, they cannot
choose)
• Key to business success: Manufacturing a large volume of inexpensive products and
services: productive efficiency
• Production function-dominated organization: Developing production capacity and
improving productivity
• Marketing plays a null or very limited and passive role (no market research, poor
communication, no interest in knowing what the customer wants, etc.)
3. Evolution in the orientation of companies towards
Marketing:
Product Orientation
• Environment: Demand BALANCED with supply, greater competition
• Assumption: People choose products that offer the best quality. Good products sell
themselves.
• Key to business success: Manufacturing quality products and services at a
reasonable cost
• Organization dominated by the production function: Concern for the technical
perfection of the product
• Marketing: a limited and passive role. Quality takes on special importance.
consequence

3. Evolution in the orientation of companies towards
Marketing:
Sales Orientation
• Environment: competitive market. Supply GREATER than Demand. New forms of
distribution (self-service), expanded markets, brand policy.
• Assumption: If you don't encourage buying, people won't buy.
• Key to business success: Convincing people to buy through advertising, personal selling,
promotions, etc.
• Product decisions linked to the production function: Concern for creating an efficient
commercial organization (creation of the Sales Department)
• Limited marketing role: focused solely on an aggressive sales effort. Offer-based
approach
3. Evolution in the orientation of companies towards
Marketing:
Market Orientation
• Environment: competitive market. Supply GREATER than Demand. Mature, fragmented
markets, new needs, globalization.
• Assumption: Principle of consumer sovereignty (“the customer is king”)
• Key to business success: Identify the needs and desires of the target audience and satisfy
them more effectively and efficiently than the competition.
• Organization dominated by a market orientation (people and competition). Demand-
driven approach, the starting point is the customer.
• Central role of marketing: identifying needs and wants, providing superior net value to
the competition

4. Trends within Market Orientation:
EXPERIENTIAL MARKETING
Experiential marketing is a marketing strategy that aims to create memorable
and exciting situations in which the consumer actively participates instead of just
being a passive recipient of advertising.
More than selling a product, the goal is to make the consumer "feel" the brand.
OBJECTIVE: for the person to remember the experience
and share it with others, which strengthens:
• Loyalty
• The recommendation
• Positive brand perception
4. Trends in Market Orientation:
Experiental Marketing
Five types of experiences that can be developed for customers: (Schmitt, 2003):
• Sensations: focused on the senses – sight, smell, hearing, taste and touch –
• Feelings: Arouse positive feelings about the brand such as love, joy, enthusiasm,
etc.
• Thoughts: relate positive thoughts to the brand, such as ecology or education.
• Actions: Motivate customers to take actions that have a positive impact on the
way they relate to the brand, even improving their lives.
• Relationships: Create experiences that connect the person who buys the product
with a social system, community, or identity.

NEUROMARKETING
Analyze what mental processes, both explicit

They allow us to determine which areas of a

Therefore, they can identify the conscious and unconscious
